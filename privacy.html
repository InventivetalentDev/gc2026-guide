Privacy

Information under Art. 13 GDPR · Last updated 17 August 2026

This guide has no accounts, login or comment fields. Your saved list, itinerary, played marks and filters stay in your browser. If you voluntarily report a live queue, that queue session is sent to the guide's small backend and kept only as described below. Visitor numbers are counted in aggregate, without cookies.

Who is responsible

Haylee Schäfer, Wiesenstraße 7, 78112 St. Georgen, Germany · mail@inventivetalent.org. Full details are in the imprint.

What stays in your browser

The guide's main interface and ordinary saved data remain on your device. Saving an exhibitor, marking a game as played, building an itinerary or changing a filter writes to localStorage under these keys:

  • gc2026.saved.v1 — exhibitors and games you saved
  • gc2026.played.v1 — games you marked as played
  • gc2026.itinerary.v1 — your planned visit
  • gc2026.prefs.v1 — view preferences, e.g. age filter and “hide played”
  • gc2026.queue.v1 — a random, resettable queue-reporting id, active local timers and a completion waiting to reconnect, if you choose to report

The saved, played, itinerary and preference entries are never transmitted to me or to anyone else. Queue data is the explicit exception: only when you use a queue-report button are the relevant fields from the queue entry sent. None of these entries is a cookie or is attached to ordinary page requests. Clearing this domain's site data deletes the local copies, and uninstalling the app or using private browsing has the same effect. I cannot read, restore or delete the local-only entries for you.

When you share your list, it is encoded into the part of the link after the #. Browsers never send that part to a server, so a shared list does not reach me, my host, or their logs — it travels only inside the link, to whoever you send it to. Opening someone else's link holds that incoming list in sessionStorage under gc2026.share.pending just long enough for you to accept or dismiss it, so a reload cannot lose the offer; unlike everything above, it is discarded the moment the tab closes.

To work in a hall with no reception, the guide also keeps a copy of itself — pages, styles, fonts, icons and the exhibitor data — in the browser's cache storage. That is the app, not information about you, and it is removed with the rest when you clear the site's data. Storing all of this is what makes the offline guide you asked for work at all, so under § 25 (2) TDDDG it needs no consent banner.

Optional live queue reports

If you choose “I'm in this queue”, the backend receives the exhibitor and game (or booth), a random resettable device UUID, the elapsed-time and people-ahead buckets you tapped, optional queue mechanics, and server timestamps. It receives outcomes such as entered, left or queue closed when you report them. It receives no name, account, precise location, contacts, saved list or itinerary. Reporting is entirely optional; reading the guide and its live aggregates does not create a queue session.

The random id keeps one device to one open session and makes basic throttling and moderation possible. It is not tied to an account, but a day of reports under one id can still sketch that device's movement through the show. You can reset it by clearing this site's data. Cloudflare uses the connection IP transiently for a loose flood limit; the guide does not store the IP with a report or in its queue database.

An active timer keeps running locally without reception. If you tap “I'm in” offline, the device stores the measured elapsed seconds and submits that one completion after connectivity returns. Stale people-ahead, mechanics and closure reports are never queued for later delivery.

The purpose is to give visitors current, crowd-sourced waiting information and protect it from obvious abuse. The legal basis is Art. 6 (1) (f) GDPR — the legitimate interests in operating this optional fan service securely and helping visitors make time-sensitive choices. Fixed coarse buckets, short retention, no account and an easily reset id limit the impact.

Hosting and delivery

The site runs on Cloudflare Workers and is delivered over Cloudflare's network (Cloudflare, Inc., 101 Townsend St., San Francisco, CA 94107, USA), which serves the files and protects the site from attacks. Cloudflare is the only provider involved in delivering this site.

Serving a web page technically requires processing connection data: your IP address, the time of the request, the page requested, the referring page, and your browser's user agent and language. This happens for every website you visit and is stored briefly in server logs for delivery, security and error diagnosis. The legal basis is Art. 6 (1) (f) GDPR — my legitimate interest in providing a working, reasonably secure site. Cloudflare acts as a processor on my behalf under Art. 28 GDPR.

Your browser may additionally report failed connections to Cloudflare's network error logging endpoint (a.nel.cloudflare.com). Successful requests are not reported.

Visitor statistics

This site uses Cloudflare Web Analytics to count how many people visit. A small script is loaded from static.cloudflareinsights.com and reports a page view.

It is deliberately chosen because of what it does not do: it sets no cookies, writes nothing to your device, does not fingerprint you, does not build a profile, and cannot follow you to other websites. There is no persistent identifier — repeat visits are not linked to each other. What is recorded is aggregate and non-identifying: page visited, referring site, country (derived from the IP address, which is not stored for this purpose), broad device and browser type, screen size, and page load timings.

I use this only to see roughly how many people find the guide useful. I cannot see individual visitors, and no attempt is made to identify anyone. The legal basis is Art. 6 (1) (f) GDPR; my legitimate interest is knowing whether the project is worth maintaining, and the impact on you is minimal because nothing is stored on your device and nothing identifies you. Because no information is stored in or read out of your terminal equipment for this, no consent banner is required under § 25 TDDDG. You can block the script with any content blocker and the guide will work exactly as before. Details are in Cloudflare's description of the service.

Transfers outside the EU

Cloudflare is a US company and may process connection data outside the EU. It is certified under the EU–US Data Privacy Framework and additionally relies on the European Commission's Standard Contractual Clauses, which is the legal basis for these transfers under Art. 44 ff. GDPR.

Links to other sites

The guide links out to gamescom's official pages, exhibitor websites and similar. Following such a link takes you to a site with its own privacy policy, over which I have no influence.

How long data is kept

Server and security logs are retained by Cloudflare for a short period, typically a few days to a few weeks, and then deleted. Analytics data is aggregated and holds nothing that could identify you. Queue sessions, updates, closure votes and moderation event data are pruned within 24 hours. Queue-mechanics votes, device-id denylist entries and the moderation audit log are kept only through the show, after which the complete queue database is deleted. Data in your browser stays until you clear it.

Your rights

You have the right to access (Art. 15), rectification (Art. 16), erasure (Art. 17), restriction of processing (Art. 18), data portability (Art. 20), and to object at any time to processing based on legitimate interest (Art. 21). To exercise any of these, write to mail@inventivetalent.org.

In practice there is a limit worth being honest about: queue reports carry only a random device id and the other server data is not tied to an identity. Unless you provide that id while it still exists, I usually have no way to locate records belonging to you, and may not be able to answer an access request beyond what is written on this page (Art. 11 GDPR).

You may also complain to a supervisory authority. The one responsible for me is the State Commissioner for Data Protection and Freedom of Information Baden-Württemberg.

Freiwillige Live-Warteschlangenmeldungen

Wenn du „Ich stehe hier an“ auswählst, erhält das Backend den Aussteller und das Spiel (oder den Stand), eine zufällige zurücksetzbare Geräte-UUID, die von dir gewählten groben Zeit- und Personenanzahl-Stufen, optionale Angaben zur Art der Schlange sowie Server-Zeitstempel. Gemeldete Ergebnisse wie „drin“, „gegangen“ oder „Schlange geschlossen“ werden ebenfalls verarbeitet. Nicht übertragen werden Name, Konto, genauer Standort, Kontakte, Merkliste oder Reiseplan. Das Melden ist vollständig freiwillig; das Lesen der Live-Zusammenfassung eröffnet keine Sitzung.

Die zufällige ID begrenzt ein Gerät auf eine offene Sitzung je Schlange und ermöglicht Missbrauchsschutz. Sie gehört zu keinem Konto. Trotzdem kann ein Tag mit Meldungen unter derselben ID grob zeigen, welche Schlangen dieses Gerät besucht hat. Durch Löschen der Website-Daten kannst du die ID zurücksetzen. Cloudflare verarbeitet die Verbindungs-IP vorübergehend für eine lockere Flutbegrenzung; der Guide speichert die IP weder bei Meldungen noch in seiner Warteschlangen-Datenbank.

Ein laufender Timer funktioniert ohne Empfang weiter. Tippst du offline auf „Ich bin drin“, speichert das Gerät die gemessenen Sekunden und sendet genau diesen Abschluss nach Wiederherstellung der Verbindung. Veraltete Personenanzahlen, Mechanik- und Schließungsmeldungen werden nie später nachgesendet.

Zweck ist eine aktuelle, gemeinschaftlich erfasste Warteinformation und ihr Schutz vor einfachem Missbrauch. Rechtsgrundlage ist Art. 6 Abs. 1 lit. f DSGVO — das berechtigte Interesse, diesen freiwilligen Fan-Dienst sicher zu betreiben und zeitkritische Entscheidungen auf der Messe zu erleichtern. Grobe feste Auswahlstufen, kurze Aufbewahrung, kein Konto und eine leicht zurücksetzbare ID begrenzen die Auswirkungen.
